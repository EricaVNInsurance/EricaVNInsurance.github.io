What about dental, vision, and hearing?

Some Medicare Advantage plans include extra benefits. The details vary.

People and situations in the examples are fictional. Dollar amounts are made up to explain the ideas, not a plan quote.

What should I ask?

Check exactly what the plan pays for. A benefit name alone does not tell you how much help you will get.

  • Which services are included?
  • Which dentists, eye doctors, or other providers can I use?
  • What will I have to pay?
  • Is there a limit on what the plan pays?
  • Do I have to meet extra requirements?
Example: James needs dental work

James needs dental work and sees that a plan advertises a dental benefit. Before counting on it, he checks whether his exact treatment and dentist are covered and how much of the bill would be his.

Example: James checks the dental limit

Imagine James’s proposed dental work costs $1,600, and his plan pays up to $1,000 for certain covered dental services. He might expect to owe only $600, but the limit alone does not tell him that.

James first checks whether the treatment and dentist are covered, his required share of the cost, and how much of the benefit he has already used. These amounts are made up, not a plan quote.

What else matters?

Check your regular doctors, medicines, and medical costs too. A benefit offered by one plan or in one county may not be available with another. Use the plan’s current benefit summary and coverage documents.
