Can I have Medicare and Medicaid?

Yes, if you qualify for both. The kind of Medicaid help you have matters.

What is the difference?

Medicare is federal health insurance. Medicaid is run by states under federal rules. It helps eligible people with limited income and resources. When you have both, Medicare generally pays first for Medicare-covered care.

Does everyone get the same help?

No. Some people have full Medicaid coverage. Others have help with certain Medicare costs through a Medicare Savings Program. Extra Help is a separate program for prescription costs. Having Extra Help alone does not mean you have full Medicaid.

What do the different Medicaid “levels” mean?

When someone has Medicare, you may hear their assistance described as a “level.” These are eligibility categories: they describe the type of help someone has. They are not a rating of how sick someone is, and they do not form one simple ladder from lowest to highest.

This section explains the seven main Medicare-related assistance categories. Medicaid also has other coverage groups for people who do not have Medicare, such as children, pregnant people, and some adults. A state may use additional names or codes.

What does the plus (+) mean?

QMB+ = QMB assistance + full Medicaid benefits.

SLMB+ = SLMB assistance + full Medicaid benefits.

“Only” means that assistance category does not also include full Medicaid benefits. “Full” means the Medicaid benefit package for the group you qualify under—not that every service is free or covered.

Full-benefit categories: QMB+, SLMB+, and other full-benefit Medicaid. Partial-benefit categories: QMB-only, SLMB-only, QI, and QDWI. “Partial” refers to the Medicaid assistance package, not a fraction of Medicare coverage.

QMB-only

Qualified Medicare Beneficiary. Helps with Part A premiums when owed, Part B premiums, and Medicare-covered Part A and Part B deductibles, coinsurance, and copayments.

Full Medicaid benefits? No. QMB-only is help with Medicare costs, rather than the full Medicaid benefit package.

QMB+ (QMB Plus)

The same QMB assistance and Medicare billing protections, plus full Medicaid benefits for the state coverage group you qualify under.

Full Medicaid benefits? Yes. Additional Medicaid-covered services still have their own rules.

QMB-only still protects you from Medicare cost-sharing bills.

Both QMB-only and QMB+ have this protection: Medicare providers and suppliers cannot bill you for Medicare-covered Part A and Part B deductibles, coinsurance, or copayments. This applies with Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage, even if the provider does not participate in Medicaid or Medicaid pays the provider nothing.

A small Medicaid copayment may apply in certain circumstances. The protection does not turn an uncovered service into a covered one. If you get a bill, tell the provider you have QMB and ask them to review it. Read the official QMB billing protections.

Example: QMB does not need a plus to protect Medicare cost sharing.

Rosa has QMB-only. Daniel has QMB+. Both receive a Medicare-covered doctor visit. Neither can be billed the Medicare coinsurance for that visit. Daniel also has his state’s full Medicaid benefits; Rosa does not have that additional benefit package through QMB-only.

Fictional example. Coverage rules still apply.

SLMB-only

Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary. Helps pay the Part B premium.

Full Medicaid benefits? No. SLMB-only does not provide QMB’s Medicare cost-sharing protection. You can still owe medical deductibles, coinsurance, or copayments, depending on your other coverage.

SLMB+ (SLMB Plus)

Part B premium help through SLMB, plus full Medicaid benefits for your state coverage group.

Full Medicaid benefits? Yes. For Medicaid-covered care furnished by a Medicaid provider, the Medicaid payment rules limit what you may owe. This is a different category from QMB+; do not assume every Medicare-only service has the same protection as QMB.

Example: Part B help and full Medicaid are different.

Elena’s notice says SLMB-only. It helps with her Part B premium, but does not mean all her doctor bills are covered. If a later notice says SLMB+, she also has full Medicaid benefits. She should check which services and providers those benefits cover.

Fictional example; a change in category requires the state’s determination.

Other full-benefit Medicaid / FBDE

Full-benefit dual eligible broadly means a person with Medicare and full Medicaid. QMB+ and SLMB+ are full-benefit categories too.

Some records use “FBDE,” “full Medicaid only,” or “other full-benefit dual” more specifically for someone who has full Medicaid but is not in the QMB or SLMB groups. Here, “only” does not mean the person lacks Medicare; it distinguishes their Medicaid category.

Full Medicaid benefits? Yes. Medicare premiums and medical cost sharing depend on the state’s rules and applicable coverage. Do not assume all Medicare-only services have a $0 bill. Ask which providers and services qualify for Medicaid payment.

QI (sometimes QI-1)

Qualifying Individual. Helps pay the Part B premium. It is for people who do not qualify for other Medicaid coverage.

Full Medicaid benefits? No. QI does not give QMB’s medical cost-sharing protection. You must apply each year. Funding is limited; states give priority to people who received QI the previous year.

QDWI

Qualified Disabled and Working Individual. Helps eligible working people with disabilities pay the Part A premium after losing premium-free Part A because they returned to work.

Full Medicaid benefits? No. QDWI itself pays the Part A premium, not Part B premiums or medical cost sharing.

Income and resource rules differ by program and state. A category cannot be identified from income alone. Check current Medicare Savings Program requirements.

Which category codes might I see on a record?

CMS uses these main dual-status codes: 01 QMB-only; 02 QMB+; 03 SLMB-only; 04 SLMB+; 05 QDWI; 06 QI; 08 other full-benefit dual.

These are Medicare-related reporting codes, not the complete list of state Medicaid aid codes. A number on a card may mean something different. Ask the agency to interpret your record.

Where does Extra Help / LIS fit in?

Extra Help, also called the Low-Income Subsidy (LIS), helps with Medicare Part D prescription costs. It is separate from these medical-assistance categories.

People with Medicare and full Medicaid, or QMB, SLMB, or QI, automatically qualify for Extra Help. QDWI alone does not automatically qualify someone. Others may qualify by applying. Having Extra Help does not, by itself, mean you have full Medicaid or QMB protection for doctor bills. A Part D subsidy or copay label is not a Medicaid category.

Are there special versions for transplant drug coverage?

Yes. You may see QMB-Part B-ID, SLMB-Part B-ID, or QI-Part B-ID. These relate to the limited Medicare immunosuppressive-drug benefit. They do not mean the person has full Medicare medical coverage. Ask the agency which premiums or drug cost sharing the assistance covers.

Are children’s Medicaid, pregnancy coverage, or long-term-care programs extra “levels”?

They are Medicaid eligibility or service pathways, not additional rungs above QMB+. A person with Medicare may qualify for full Medicaid through an age, disability, or long-term-care pathway and also have a dual-status category. Read the Medicaid basics guide for the broader picture.

How can I find out which category I have?

Read your current state eligibility notice, or contact the state Medicaid agency. Ask:

  • Do I have full Medicaid benefits, a Medicare Savings Program, or both?
  • What is my exact category—QMB-only, QMB+, SLMB-only, SLMB+, QI, QDWI, or another full-benefit category?
  • Which premiums are paid, what can I owe for medical care, and when is my next review?

A card, an old approval letter, or a Part B premium no longer being deducted from a Social Security payment does not identify every benefit. Use your current eligibility record. You do not need to share a Medicaid or Medicare identification number in this website’s request form.

What is a D-SNP?

It is a type of Medicare Advantage plan for people who meet that plan’s Medicare and Medicaid requirements. Each plan accepts certain Medicaid categories, or types of assistance. QMB+, SLMB+, and other full-benefit Medicaid do not automatically qualify someone for every D-SNP. Some plans accept partial-benefit categories; others require full Medicaid. The plan, state contract, location, and enrollment period matter.

What to check first

Confirm your current type of assistance, which plans accept it, and when you are allowed to sign up. A Medicaid card alone does not answer all three questions.
