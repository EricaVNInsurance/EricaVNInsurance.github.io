Costs

What might I have to pay?

There are costs to keep your coverage and costs when you use it.

People and situations in the examples are fictional. Dollar amounts are made up to explain the ideas, not a plan quote.

Four words that explain the costs

Premium: your monthly payment
You pay it to keep coverage, even in months when you do not use care.
Deductible: an amount you pay first
For services or medicines subject to a deductible, you pay toward this amount before coverage starts paying. Some care may be covered before you meet it.
Copay: a set dollar amount
For example, a plan might charge a set amount for a doctor visit.
Coinsurance: your percentage
You pay part of a covered bill as a percentage, such as 20%.
Coinsurance example: James pays a percentage

James has a covered doctor visit with a plan-approved cost of $150. He has met the deductible that applies, and his coinsurance is 20%.

James pays $30, and coverage pays $120. These amounts are made up, not a plan quote.

Deductible example: Daniel finishes his deductible

Daniel has $100 left to meet a deductible that applies to his next covered service. That service has an approved cost of $200.

He pays the first $100 to finish the deductible. His coinsurance is then 20% of the remaining $100, so he pays another $20. Daniel pays $120 total; coverage pays $80. These amounts are made up, not a plan quote.

Copay example: Elena visits her doctor

Elena’s plan has a $25 copay for a covered doctor visit, with no deductible applying to that visit. Her share is $25.

A different type of visit may have a different copay. The $25 is made up, not a plan quote.

Does a lower monthly payment mean a lower total cost?

Not always. Add up the monthly payments and what you may pay for visits, hospital care, and medicines.

Example: Maria looks beyond the monthly premium

Maria is comparing two plans. One has a $0 monthly plan premium and $900 in estimated costs for visits and treatments. The other has a $30 monthly plan premium ($360 a year) and $400 in estimated costs for visits and treatments.

For those costs, the totals are $900 and $760. Maria still needs to add Part B, medicines, and any other costs that apply. These amounts are made up. Estimates do not guarantee what she will spend.

Is there a yearly limit?

Original Medicare alone has no yearly cap on your share of medical costs. Medicare Advantage sets a limit for covered Part A and Part B services. Drug costs have separate rules. Monthly payments and care that is not covered generally do not count toward the medical limit.

Costs can change each year. Check the year and the exact plan before comparing prices.
