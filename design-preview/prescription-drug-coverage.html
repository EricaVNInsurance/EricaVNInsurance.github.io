Prescription coverage

How does Part D work?

The plan name alone cannot tell you what your prescriptions will cost.

Two common ways to get it

People with Original Medicare can join a separate Part D plan. Most Medicare Advantage plans include drug coverage. In most Medicare Advantage plan types, you cannot simply add a separate Part D plan.

Check your actual medications

Each plan has a covered drug list called a formulary. Use the exact medication name, strength, and how often you take it when comparing. Check your pharmacy and estimated annual costs, too.

Look for coverage rules

  • Prior authorization: The plan requires approval before covering certain prescriptions.
  • Step therapy: You may need to try a specified drug first, unless an exception is approved.
  • Quantity limits: The plan limits how much it covers over a period of time.

If a medication is not covered or has restrictions, ask the plan and your prescriber about the coverage determination or exception process.

Even if you take no medications now

Check your drug coverage options. Going without Part D or other creditable drug coverage can lead to a late enrollment penalty.
